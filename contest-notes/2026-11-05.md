## 1. Muse Spark Open Kernel Challenge (Paradigm with Meta and Andromeda)

Sources: https://openkernelchallenge.dev/llms.txt, https://openkernelchallenge.dev/api/v1/serving,
https://openkernelchallenge.dev/api/v1/leaderboard, https://www.paradigm.xyz/writing/muse-spark-open-kernel-challenge

| Item | What the official sources say |
|---|---|
| Kernel track | 22 Oct to 4 Nov 2026 11:59 pm PT. Closed. Its boards carry **no prizes**. 2,014 teams are on the overall board. |
| Serving track | Opens 10 Nov 2026 9 am PT and closes 19 Nov 2026 11:59 pm PT (the API's schedule says 2026-11-20). Entries are SGLang patches (unified diff, only under `python/sglang/`, not the model loader) against commit `a55ab7d5`. |
| Prizes | $100,000, **serving board only**: $50,000 plus a signed H200 / $30,000 / $10,000 / $3,000 / $2,000 / $1,000 each for 6th to 10th. |
| Scoring | Throughput at stock SGLang's interactivity, against stock SGLang on the same 8xH200 node. Concurrency 1, 4, 16 and 32. Final places come from medians of reruns after the challenge, including held-out data. SGLang maintainers' view of whether a patch could be merged counts toward picking the winners. The board stays hidden until the end. |
| Fixed setup | NVFP4 816B-A42B model, TP 8 / EP 8, marlin MoE backend, DFlash drafter (8 draft tokens; may be sped up but not changed or swapped), FP8 KV cache, no precision below stock. Any other drafting may use only the requests themselves (prompt lookup, n-grams). |
| Limits | 1 scored serving submission and 3 checks a day. A check runs at concurrency 16, has a 35-minute deadline and rescores 8 conversations. |
| Compute | No development compute is described. Submissions run on the organisers' H200s with no network access. |
| AI agents | Agents may submit, but a person must run `okc login` in a terminal, accept the rules and complete GitHub's device flow. "An agent doesn't accept them for anyone." |
| Licence | Entries are Apache-2.0 (SGLang's licence). |

Our reading: the money is all in a 9-day serving track. Entrants with no H200s of their own
can only test through 3 checks a day, against hundreds of GPU-kernel teams. MV2 is not entering.

## 2. GenAI for Good Challenge: Cities Edition (IEEE HT with UNDP; technical assessment on Zindi)

Sources: https://api.zindi.world/v1/competitions/GenAI-for-Good-Challenge,
https://ieeeht.org/get-involved/funding-opportunities/genai-for-good/

| Item | What the official sources say |
|---|---|
| Task (Zindi, Phase 1) | Question answering with citations over reports, spreadsheets, guidance, maps and spatial data for three city use cases (Pristina transit routes, Rivne building energy, Skopje green cadastre). The system has to say when the information is insufficient. |
| Scoring | An LLM judge scores each required answer element for correctness (1, 0.5 or 0) and evidence (1, 0.5 or 0). Question score = mean of 0.8 x correctness + 0.2 x correctness x evidence. Scores are averaged by case, then by domain, then equally over the three domains. |
| Compute limits | One generative model with **no more than 3 billion parameters** (quantisation does not lower the count). At most **15 model calls and 12,000 generated tokens per question**, counting thinking, retries and self-checks. Retrieval, OCR, embeddings, reranking, GIS and calculators are allowed if they call no other generative model. No AutoML. |
| Open source | Public open-source packages and openly available pretrained models only. No paid services. Code must be released under a permissive licence (Apache 2.0, MIT or equivalent). |
| Who may enter | "Informal groups of at least two (2) individuals"; companies and organisations are ineligible. All members must be 18 or older. Legal residents of the US and other places where local law permits; OFAC-sanctioned jurisdictions are excluded. Teams commit to the year-long sprint, prototype and pilot phases and must build on ITU's GENIE.AI framework. |
| Money | No cash for the Zindi phase (5,000 Zindi points). The programme offers up to $40,000 per winning team: $10,000 to build a prototype plus up to $30,000 to pilot it with a city. The top 100 on Zindi's private leaderboard go to a panel that scores a narrative proposal: technical approach 35%, impact 25%, use-case fit 20%, team 20%. |
| Dates | Opened 5 Nov 2026. Requests to use extra datasets are due 30 Nov. Zindi closes 13 Dec 2026 04:29 UTC, and the narrative proposal is due 13 Dec 11:59 pm ET. Live Q&A on 19 Nov, 11:00 am ET. Acceptance is announced before 28 Feb 2027. |
| Submissions | 1 a day, 30 in total. Markdown, with every test ID exactly once. |
| Code sharing | No private sharing outside a team during the contest; anything shared has to go to all participants on Zindi's discussion board. |

Our reading: the task (cited answers from a small open model on mixed documents) matches
the retrieval and citation work in this repository's `spark-pubmed/` folder. Entry depends on
forming a team of at least two eligible adults, because companies cannot enter. Contest-specific
code is not shared here before the contest closes, to respect the code-sharing rule.
